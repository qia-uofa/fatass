# Equations of the Experiment

A self-contained formalization of the experiment as actually implemented in
`code_morph` (the `vonto` package plus the `notebooks_benzon` pipeline:
`1_dataset` → `2_sentiments` → `3_heatmaps` → `4_steering`). The scope is
deliberately narrower than the paper: `code_morph` implements **dataset
preparation**, **answer generation (Phase 0)**, **categorical self-report
elicitation (Phase 1)**, **computed ground truths**, the **self-report ×
ground-truth calibration sweep**, and **activation steering** (the paper's
Experiment 1). Patching, noising, swap, OOD, probing/variance partitioning,
attention blocking, and the generalization suite are *not* implemented here
and are therefore not formalized below.

Where `code_morph` follows the paper, the paper's own notation is kept:
$h^{(\ell)}_p$ for residual-stream activations, $\mu(\cdot)$ for a mean over a
set of activations, $v$ for a steering vector, $\alpha$ for steering strength,
$\Delta_{\mathrm{logit}}$ for the logit-difference metric.

---

## 0. Preliminaries

### 0.1 Model and forward pass

Let $\mathcal{V}$ be the tokenizer vocabulary of the single chat model used
throughout (`Qwen/Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct`, loaded in bf16, eval mode, gradients
disabled), and let $\Sigma^{*}$ denote the set of finite strings.

* $\mathrm{TOK} : \Sigma^{*} \to \mathcal{V}^{*}$ — tokenization without special
  tokens; $\mathrm{DEC} : \mathcal{V}^{*} \to \Sigma^{*}$ — its decoding inverse.
* $\mathrm{OFF} : \Sigma^{*} \to (\mathbb{N}\times\mathbb{N})^{*}$ — the
  offset mapping, $\mathrm{OFF}(u)_i = (b_i, e_i)$ being the half-open
  character span $[b_i, e_i)$ of $u$ covered by token $i$.
* $\mathrm{CHAT} : \Sigma^{*} \to \Sigma^{*}$ — the chat template applied to a
  single user turn with `add_generation_prompt=True`.
* $\oplus$ — string concatenation; $|u|$ — the length of a string or sequence.

The transformer has $L$ decoder layers of width $d$. For a token sequence
$x \in \mathcal{V}^{*}$ write

$$
h^{(\ell)}_{p}(x) \in \mathbb{R}^{d}, \qquad \ell \in \{0,\dots,L-1\},\ p \in \{0,\dots,|x|-1\}
\tag{P1}
$$

for the residual-stream activation at token position $p$ on the **output** of
decoder layer $\ell$ (the tensor a `forward_hook` on `model.model.layers[l]`
observes), and

$$
z(x) \;=\; \text{final-layer logits of the last position of } x \;\in\; \mathbb{R}^{|\mathcal{V}|}
\tag{P2}
$$

for the next-token logits. All reads in this experiment are last-position
reads, so $z(x)$ is the only logit object needed.

### 0.2 Standard functions

For $u \in \mathbb{R}^{n}$ and a finite multiset $A = \{a_1,\dots,a_N\}$:

$$
\mathrm{softmax}(u)_i = \frac{e^{u_i}}{\sum_{j} e^{u_j}},
\qquad
\mathrm{logistic}(u) = \frac{1}{1+e^{-u}},
\qquad
\cos(u,w) = \frac{u^{\top} w}{\lVert u\rVert\,\lVert w\rVert},
\tag{P3}
$$

$$
\overline{A} = \frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N} a_i,
\qquad
\mathrm{sem}(A) = \begin{cases}
\sqrt{\dfrac{1}{N(N-1)}\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{N}\bigl(a_i - \overline{A}\bigr)^{2}}, & N > 1,\\[2ex]
\mathrm{NaN}, & N \le 1 .
\end{cases}
\tag{P4}
$$

$\mathbf{1}[\,\cdot\,]$ is the indicator function, $\lVert\cdot\rVert$ the
Euclidean norm, $\lceil\cdot\rceil$/$\lfloor\cdot\rfloor$ ceiling/floor, and
$\mathcal{U}(S)$ the uniform distribution on $S$.

### 0.3 Seeds, inquiries, trials

The pipeline is organized around three record types, in strict order.

A **dataset** $D$ is a finite ordered list of **seeds**
$\mathcal{S}(D) = (s_1,\dots,s_{|D|})$, plus a map $s \mapsto \mathcal{I}(s)$
turning one seed into an **inquiry**

$$
\mathcal{I} = (q,\ \tau,\ \zeta), \qquad q \in \Sigma^{*},\ \ \tau \in [0,1),\ \ \zeta \in \mathbb{N}\cup\{\bot\},
\tag{P5}
$$

where $q$ is the question text actually put to the model, $\tau$ its sampling
temperature ($\tau = 0$ meaning greedy decoding) and $\zeta$ the generation
seed ($\bot$ = none).

Running one inquiry against the model yields a **trial**

$$
\theta = \bigl(s,\ \mathcal{I},\ a,\ \mathbf{g},\ \mathbf{H}\bigr),
\tag{P6}
$$

with $a \in \Sigma^{*}$ the generated answer text,
$\mathbf{g} = (g_1,\dots,g_{n_{\mathrm{tok}}}) \in \mathbb{R}^{n_{\mathrm{tok}}}$ its per-answer-token
log-probabilities and $\mathbf{H} = (H_1,\dots,H_{n_{\mathrm{tok}}}) \in \mathbb{R}_{\ge 0}^{n_{\mathrm{tok}}}$
its per-step next-token entropies, $n_{\mathrm{tok}}$ being the number of answer
tokens.
$\Theta$ denotes a finite pool of trials, $N = |\Theta|$.

### 0.4 Observation methods and ground truths

An **observation method** $\omega$ is a categorical (Likert) self-report with
an ordered class list

$$
C^{\omega} = (c^{\omega}_1,\dots,c^{\omega}_K), \qquad K = |C^{\omega}| = 10,
\tag{P7}
$$

ordered low $\to$ high, and induces two functions on a trial: the winning class
index $\hat{y}_{\omega}(\theta) \in \{1,\dots,K\}$ and the reported value
$m_{\omega}(\theta) \in [0,1]$ (its class midpoint), both defined in §3.

A **ground truth** $\gamma$ is a computed scalar $\gamma(\theta) \in \mathbb{R}$,
by convention in $[0,1]$ (a binary ground truth simply takes values in
$\{0,1\}$), so that a self-report and its computed counterpart are directly
comparable. The concrete methods implemented are

$$
\Omega = \{\omega_{\mathrm{conf}},\ \omega_{\mathrm{comm}},\ \omega_{\mathrm{nuan}},\ \omega_{\mathrm{chal}},\ \omega_{\mathrm{var}},\ \omega_{\mathrm{imp}}\},
\tag{P8}
$$

$$
\Gamma = \{\gamma_{\mathrm{corr}},\ \gamma_{\mathrm{prob}},\ \gamma_{\mathrm{ent}},\ \gamma_{\mathrm{chal}},\ \gamma_{\mathrm{lvar}},\ \gamma_{\mathrm{imp}},\ \gamma_{\mathrm{temp}}\},
\tag{P9}
$$

for confidence, commitment, nuance, challenge, variety, impurity (observation
methods) and correctness, answer-probability, answer-entropy, challenge,
list-variance, impurity, temperature (ground truths).

### 0.5 Studied token positions

The four positions steered in a Phase-1 self-report prompt, plus the Phase-0
position AC, are

$$
\mathcal{P} = \{\mathrm{PANL},\ \mathrm{PANL{+}1},\ \mathrm{FCC},\ \mathrm{CC}\},
\qquad
\mathrm{AC} \notin \mathcal{P}.
\tag{P10}
$$

PANL is the newline immediately after the answer, PANL+1 the token after it,
FCC the colon inside the instruction block's format example, CC the final
(confidence-colon) token at which every class read is taken, and AC the last
token of the Phase-0 prompt. AC is excluded from $\mathcal{P}$ because it lives
in a different forward pass and requires a different intervention mechanism
(§6.8). Their token indices are defined in (62) and (77).

## 1. Phase 0a — Dataset preparation

### 1.1 The shared keyword universe

Ten WordNet noun synsets form the category universe $\mathcal{C}$ (seven
concrete, three abstract):

$$
\mathcal{C} = \{\text{animal.n.01},\ \text{plant.n.02},\ \text{food.n.01},\ \text{body\_part.n.01},\ \text{vehicle.n.01},\
\text{structure.n.01},\ \text{container.n.01},\ \text{emotion.n.01},\ \text{cognition.n.01},\ \text{attribute.n.02}\},
$$

$$
\mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{abs}} = \{\text{emotion.n.01},\ \text{cognition.n.01},\ \text{attribute.n.02}\} \subset \mathcal{C}, \qquad |\mathcal{C}| = 10 .
\tag{1}
$$

*What it is.* The fixed category set every procedurally generated dataset draws
from; $\mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{abs}}$ marks the three categories with no physical
referent, available for a concrete-vs-abstract split.

Keyword frequency is read off the Brown corpus rather than hand-picked. Let
$B$ be the multiset of Brown part-of-speech-tagged tokens $(w,\mathrm{tag})$:

$$
\mathrm{cnt}(w) \;=\; \bigl|\{(w',\mathrm{tag}) \in B \;:\; \mathrm{lower}(w') = w,\ \mathrm{tag} \in \{\texttt{NN},\texttt{NNS}\},\ w' \text{ alphabetic},\ |w'| \ge 3 \}\bigr| .
\tag{2}
$$

*What it computes.* The common-noun token frequency of the lower-cased word
form $w$ in written English. $\mathcal{W} = \{w : \mathrm{cnt}(w) > 0\}$ is the
resulting word universe.

WordNet membership is by hyponym transitive closure. For a synset $C$, write
$\mathrm{hyp}(C)$ for its direct hyponyms and

$$
\mathrm{Clo}(C) \;=\; \{C\} \cup \bigcup_{k \ge 1} \mathrm{hyp}^{k}(C),
\qquad
\mathrm{Mem}(C) \;=\; \bigl\{\, w \in \mathcal{W} \;:\; \mathrm{syn}_{\mathrm{n}}(w) \cap \mathrm{Clo}(C) \neq \emptyset \,\bigr\},
\tag{3}
$$

where $\mathrm{syn}_{\mathrm{n}}(w)$ is the set of noun senses of $w$.

*What it computes.* $\mathrm{Clo}(C)$ is every synset at or below $C$ in the
hyponymy hierarchy; $\mathrm{Mem}(C)$ is the set of Brown common nouns having
at least one noun sense inside it — i.e. the members of category $C$.

Keywords are then drawn **weighted by natural frequency, without replacement**.
For a category $C$ and a draw size $n$, define the frequency distribution
restricted to $\mathrm{Mem}(C)$,

$$
P_{C}(w) \;=\; \frac{\mathrm{cnt}(w)}{\sum_{w' \in \mathrm{Mem}(C)} \mathrm{cnt}(w')},
\qquad w \in \mathrm{Mem}(C),
\tag{4}
$$

and draw $\mathrm{sample}(C,n) = (w_1,\dots,w_{n'})$, $n' = \min(n, |\mathrm{Mem}(C)|)$,
sequentially with renormalization:

$$
\Pr\bigl[w_j = w \,\big|\, w_1,\dots,w_{j-1}\bigr]
\;=\;
\frac{P_{C}(w)\;\mathbf{1}\bigl[w \notin \{w_1,\dots,w_{j-1}\}\bigr]}{1 - \sum_{i<j} P_{C}(w_i)} .
\tag{5}
$$

*Why.* Sampling proportional to real usage frequency (rather than from a
hand-written list) keeps the keyword universe representative of English;
without-replacement drawing guarantees no repeats *within* one category's draw
(two different categories may still legitimately share a word). The whole draw
is a deterministic function of an integer RNG seed.

A generated or observed word can also be classified back into the universe:

$$
\mathrm{cat}(w) \;=\;
\begin{cases}
\text{the first } C \in \mathcal{C} \text{ with } \mathrm{syn}_{\mathrm{n}}(w) \cap \mathrm{Clo}(C) \neq \emptyset, \\
\bot \quad \text{if no such } C \text{ exists.}
\end{cases}
\tag{6}
$$

*What it is.* The membership test of (3) applied to an arbitrary word rather
than to a Brown-corpus word — used to check how on-topic a generated item is
relative to its own seed category. $\bot$ denotes "no category".

### 1.2 TriviaQA (downloaded)

Let $R$ be the raw `rc.nocontext` validation split, each row carrying a question
id, a question string and an alias tuple. Define the question normalizer

$$
\mathrm{nrm}_{q}(u) \;=\; \text{whitespace-collapsed}\bigl(\mathrm{lower}(\mathrm{strip}(u))\bigr),
\tag{7}
$$

and keep the first occurrence of each normalized question, truncating at a
limit $n_{\mathrm{tqa}}$ (`triviaqa.limit` $= 200$):

$$
\mathcal{S}(D_{\mathrm{tqa}}) \;=\; \Bigl(\, s_i = (q_i,\ e_i,\ A_i) \;\Bigm|\; i \le n_{\mathrm{tqa}},\ \ \mathrm{nrm}_{q}(q_i) \neq \mathrm{nrm}_{q}(q_j)\ \forall j < i \,\Bigr),
\qquad e_i = A_{i,1},
\tag{8}
$$

*What it computes.* The deduplicated seed list. $q_i$ is the raw trivia
question, $A_i = (A_{i,1},\dots)$ the tuple of all accepted gold aliases, and
$e_i$ the **reference answer** — the first alias — used wherever a single
answer string is needed. Deduplication is required because the raw split
repeats questions verbatim.

Two hardcoded phrasings turn a $(q,e)$ pair into an asked question:

$$
\mathrm{DQ}(q,e) = q,
\qquad
\mathrm{BJ}(q,e) = \texttt{Is the answer to "}\,q\,\texttt{" "}\,e\,\texttt{"? Answer Yes or No.}
\tag{9}
$$

$$
\mathcal{I}(s_i) \;=\; \bigl(\, \Phi(q_i, e_i),\ \tau = 0,\ \zeta = 0 \,\bigr), \qquad \Phi \in \{\mathrm{DQ},\ \mathrm{BJ}\},
\tag{10}
$$

*What it is.* The swappable question phrasing $\Phi$ (default $\mathrm{DQ}$ in
every notebook run) and the resulting inquiry. $\tau = 0$ implements the
paper's greedy, temperature-0 requirement for the calibration datasets.

### 1.3 Synonyms (lexical-relation benchmarks)

Let $\mathrm{LRC}(d,\mathrm{sp})$ be the set of $(\text{head},\text{tail},\text{relation})$
triples in split $\mathrm{sp}$ of benchmark $d$ of
`relbert/lexical_relation_classification`. Three relation tiers are pooled:

$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathcal{Q}_{\mathrm{twin}}^{0} &= \{(h,t) : (h,t,\texttt{Synonym}) \in \mathrm{LRC}(\text{EVALution},\text{train})\}
\;\cup\!\!\bigcup_{\mathrm{sp} \in \{\text{train},\text{test}\}}\!\! \{(h,t) : (h,t,\texttt{SYN}) \in \mathrm{LRC}(\text{CogALexV},\mathrm{sp})\},\\
\mathcal{Q}_{\mathrm{sib}} &= \bigcup_{\mathrm{sp}\in\{\text{train},\text{val},\text{test}\}} \{(h,t) : (h,t,\texttt{sibl}) \in \mathrm{LRC}(\text{K\&H+N},\mathrm{sp})\},\\
\mathcal{Q}_{\mathrm{rel}} &= \{(h,t) : (h,t,r) \in \mathrm{LRC}(\text{BLESS},\text{train}),\ r \in \{\texttt{hyper},\texttt{mero},\texttt{attri},\texttt{event}\}\}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{11}
$$

*What they are.* The candidate pools for the three tiers: **twin**
(interchangeable), **sibling** (co-hyponyms: same meaning, not interchangeable)
and **relative** (related but neither synonymous nor co-hyponymous — BLESS's
own `coord` and `random` relations are deliberately excluded). Each pool is
deduplicated as a set.

Let $E : \Sigma^{*} \rightharpoonup \mathbb{R}^{300}$ be the GoogleNews
word2vec table, keyed by lower-cased tokens, read directly from the raw binary
format. Define an out-of-vocabulary-tolerant similarity

$$
\mathrm{sim}(u,w) \;=\;
\begin{cases}
\cos\bigl(E[\mathrm{lower}(u)],\, E[\mathrm{lower}(w)]\bigr), & \mathrm{lower}(u),\mathrm{lower}(w) \in \mathrm{dom}(E),\\
-1, & \text{otherwise,}
\end{cases}
\tag{12}
$$

so that an OOV pair is always rejected by the twin filter

$$
\mathcal{Q}_{\mathrm{twin}} \;=\; \bigl\{\,(h,t) \in \mathcal{Q}_{\mathrm{twin}}^{0} \;:\; \mathrm{sim}(h,t) \ge \varepsilon_{\mathrm{sim}} \,\bigr\}, \qquad \varepsilon_{\mathrm{sim}} = 0.25 .
\tag{13}
$$

*Why.* A pair merely *labeled* "Synonym" is not automatically interchangeable
in every context; requiring a real embedding similarity of at least
$\varepsilon_{\mathrm{sim}}$ rejects the loosest labeled pairs.
$\varepsilon_{\mathrm{sim}}$ is
`min_twin_similarity`.

Seeds are then drawn uniformly without replacement, $n_{\mathrm{syn}} = 67$ per
tier (`synonyms.n`):

$$
\mathcal{S}(D_{\mathrm{syn}}) \;=\; \bigsqcup_{r \in \{\mathrm{twin},\,\mathrm{sib},\,\mathrm{rel}\}} \Bigl\{\, s = \bigl((h,t),\, r\bigr) \;:\; (h,t) \sim \mathcal{U}\bigl(\mathcal{Q}_{r}\bigr)\ \text{w/o repl.},\ \min\bigl(n_{\mathrm{syn}}, |\mathcal{Q}_{r}|\bigr) \text{ draws} \Bigr\},
\tag{14}
$$

with reference answer and inquiry

$$
e(s) = \begin{cases}\texttt{Yes}, & r = \mathrm{twin}\\ \texttt{No}, & r \in \{\mathrm{sib}, \mathrm{rel}\}\end{cases}
\qquad
\mathcal{I}(s) = \Bigl(\ \texttt{Do "}h\texttt{" and "}t\texttt{" mean the same thing?},\ \ \tau = 0,\ \ \zeta = 0\ \Bigr).
\tag{15}
$$

*Why.* Only twin pairs genuinely mean the same thing, so $e(s)$ is the correct
Yes/No answer; exposing it under the same name every other seed uses lets the
correctness and challenge ground truths grade this dataset with no
per-dataset special-casing.

### 1.4 List elicitation

For each category and each sampled keyword, $T_{\mathrm{temp}}$ independent
seeds are drawn, each with its own sampling temperature and generation seed:

$$
\mathcal{S}(D_{\mathrm{list}}) \;=\;
\Bigl(\, s = (w,\ \tau,\ \zeta) \;:\; C \in \mathcal{C},\ w \in \mathrm{sample}(C, n_{\mathrm{kw}}),\ j = 1,\dots,T_{\mathrm{temp}} \,\Bigr),
\quad
\tau \sim \mathcal{U}\bigl([0,\,0.8)\bigr),\ \ \zeta \sim \mathcal{U}\bigl(\{0,\dots,2^{31}-1\}\bigr),
\tag{16}
$$

$$
\bigl|\mathcal{S}(D_{\mathrm{list}})\bigr| \;=\; |\mathcal{C}| \cdot n_{\mathrm{kw}} \cdot T_{\mathrm{temp}}
\qquad (n_{\mathrm{kw}} = 1,\ T_{\mathrm{temp}} = 20 \Rightarrow 200),
$$

$$
\mathcal{I}(s) \;=\; \Bigl(\ \texttt{Give me a list of } \kappa \texttt{ things, starting with } w \texttt{.},\ \ \tau,\ \ \zeta \ \Bigr), \qquad \kappa = 20 .
\tag{17}
$$

*Variables.* $n_{\mathrm{kw}}$ = keywords sampled per category
(`list_elicitation.n`); $T_{\mathrm{temp}}$ = temperatures per keyword
(`list_elicitation.T`); $\kappa$ = number of list items requested
(`list_elicitation.k`).

*Why $T_{\mathrm{temp}} > 1$.* Several temperatures **per keyword** are what
allow a temperature-vs-variety correlation to be computed *within* a keyword
(§5.3), controlling for that keyword's own inherent diversity, instead of only
pooled across keywords where the two effects are confounded.

### 1.5 Twenty questions (played out at generation time)

Each seed is a fully played game. For category $C$ and game index $i$, an
index-derived RNG (so a resumed run reproduces a from-scratch run exactly)
draws the candidate universe and the secret:

$$
\mathcal{K}_{i} = \mathrm{sample}\bigl(C,\ W\bigr), \qquad
\mathrm{sec}_{i} = \mathcal{K}_{i,\,J}, \quad J \sim \mathcal{U}\bigl(\{1,\dots,W\}\bigr), \qquad W = 128 .
\tag{18}
$$

The game is then played for $T_{\mathrm{turn}}$ turns. Write $R_{0} = \mathcal{K}_{i}$
for the initially remaining candidates. At turn $t = 1,\dots,T_{\mathrm{turn}}$
the model greedily generates a question $Q_t$ from the running transcript, and a
second greedy generation is parsed into a Yes-set. Given the partition
generation $u$ and remaining set $R$, let $\mathcal{Y}(u)$ be the set of
comma-separated, lower-cased, punctuation-stripped words on the single line
following the `**Yes**:` marker; then

$$
\mathrm{Yes}(u, R) = \{\, w \in R : \mathrm{lower}(w) \in \mathcal{Y}(u) \,\},
\qquad
\mathrm{No}(u, R) = R \setminus \mathrm{Yes}(u, R).
\tag{19}
$$

*Why only that one line.* Restricting the parse to the remainder of the marker
line (rather than everything after the marker) prevents a model that keeps
talking past the list from having its own commas merged into the candidate
list, which would silently drop genuinely-listed candidates.

The turn's answer and the candidate update are

$$
A_t = \begin{cases}\texttt{Yes}, & \mathrm{sec}_{i} \in \mathrm{Yes}(u_t, R_{t-1}),\\ \texttt{No}, & \text{otherwise,}\end{cases}
\qquad
R_{t} = \begin{cases}\mathrm{Yes}(u_t, R_{t-1}), & A_t = \texttt{Yes},\\ \mathrm{No}(u_t, R_{t-1}), & A_t = \texttt{No},\end{cases}
\tag{20}
$$

producing the seed (game) $s = \bigl(\mathcal{K}_i,\ \mathrm{sec}_i,\ T_{\mathrm{turn}},\ ((Q_1,A_1),\dots,(Q_{T_{\mathrm{turn}}},A_{T_{\mathrm{turn}}}))\bigr)$
and

$$
\bigl|\mathcal{S}(D_{\mathrm{20q}})\bigr| = |\mathcal{C}| \cdot n_{\mathrm{games}},
\qquad
\mathcal{I}(s) = \bigl(\ \texttt{"...20 Questions...}\ \mathcal{K}_i \ \texttt{...}\ \mathrm{hist}(s)\ \texttt{...next Yes/No question?"},\ \ \tau = 0,\ \ \zeta = 0\ \bigr),
\tag{21}
$$

with $\mathrm{hist}(s)$ the newline-joined `Q: … / A: …` transcript.

*Variables.* $W$ = candidate-universe size per game (`twenty_questions.k`),
$T_{\mathrm{turn}}$ = turns played per game (`twenty_questions.T`),
$n_{\mathrm{games}}$ = games per category (`twenty_questions.n_games`).

*Why played here.* A game's own history is needed to ask its next question at
all, so there is no meaningful "unplayed seed" to defer; the inquiry (21) asks
for the *next* question given the played-out history.

## 2. Phase 0b — Answer generation

### 2.1 Trial sampling from a dataset

Calibration draws a fixed subset of seeds, deterministic in an RNG seed so a
cache hit and a fresh run see exactly the same seeds:

$$
\mathcal{S}_{n} \;=\; \bigl(\, s_{i} : i \in \mathrm{sort}(\mathcal{J}) \,\bigr),
\qquad
\mathcal{J} \sim \mathcal{U}\Bigl(\bigl\{\,\mathcal{J}' \subseteq \{1,\dots,|D|\} : |\mathcal{J}'| = \min(n, |D|)\,\bigr\}\Bigr).
\tag{22}
$$

*What it computes.* An index-sorted uniform subset of size $n$ (the
`n_trials`/`calibration.n_trials` $= 200$ parameter) without replacement.

For steering, **two disjoint pools** are drawn in one shot, so no trial's own
activation can help build the vector that later steers it:

$$
\mathcal{J} \sim \mathcal{U}\Bigl(\bigl\{\mathcal{J}' \subseteq \{1,\dots,|D|\} : |\mathcal{J}'| = n_{\mathrm{ext}} + n_{\mathrm{tc}}\bigr\}\Bigr),
\quad
\mathcal{J}_{\mathrm{ext}} = \mathcal{J}_{1:n_{\mathrm{ext}}},
\quad
\mathcal{J}_{\mathrm{tc}} = \mathcal{J}_{n_{\mathrm{ext}}+1:\,n_{\mathrm{ext}}+n_{\mathrm{tc}}},
\tag{23}
$$

$$
\mathcal{J}_{\mathrm{ext}} \cap \mathcal{J}_{\mathrm{tc}} = \emptyset,
\qquad
\text{requires } |D| \ge n_{\mathrm{ext}} + n_{\mathrm{tc}} .
$$

*Variables.* $n_{\mathrm{ext}} = 20$ (`steering.n_extraction`) extraction
trials; $n_{\mathrm{tc}} = 40$ (`steering.n_test_candidates`) test candidates.
Insufficient seeds is an error rather than a silently shrunk pool, since a
shrunk pool would make "disjoint" meaningless.

### 2.2 The Phase-0 prompt

Let $\mathrm{Instr}_{\omega}$ be observation method $\omega$'s classification
instruction block (defined in (34)) and $\mathrm{HDR}$ the shared framing
sentence `"You will be shown a question, together with an answer you previously provided before.\n\n"`.
Phase 0 always front-loads **confidence**'s instructions, whatever self-report
is later elicited:

$$
\Pi_{0}(q) \;=\; \mathrm{Instr}_{\omega_{\mathrm{conf}}} \oplus \texttt{"\textbackslash n\textbackslash n"} \oplus \mathrm{HDR} \oplus \texttt{"Question: "} \oplus q \oplus \texttt{"\textbackslash n"},
\tag{24}
$$

$$
x_{0}(q) \;=\; \mathrm{TOK}\bigl(\ \mathrm{CHAT}(\Pi_{0}(q)) \oplus \texttt{"**Answer**:"}\ \bigr) .
\tag{25}
$$

*What it is.* The tokenized Phase-0 context. The literal cue `**Answer**:`
is appended **after** chat-templating, so the assistant turn is *prefilled* and
the model's very next token is the first answer token; its last position is
the AC (answer-colon) position of §6.8. One canonical Phase-0 framing serves
every later Phase-1 self-report.

### 2.3 Decoding

Let $x^{(i)} = x_0(q) \oplus (t_1,\dots,t_{i-1})$ be the context before step
$i$ and $z_i = z(x^{(i)}) \in \mathbb{R}^{|\mathcal{V}|}$ the raw next-token
logits there. Tokens are produced by

$$
t_{i} \;\sim\;
\begin{cases}
\delta\bigl[\,\operatorname*{argmax}_{t \in \mathcal{V}} z_{i,t}\,\bigr], & \tau = 0 \quad (\text{greedy}),\\[1ex]
\mathrm{softmax}\bigl(z_{i}/\tau\bigr), & \tau > 0 \quad (\text{full-support sampling: top-}p = 1,\ \text{top-}k = 0),
\end{cases}
\tag{26}
$$

seeded by $\zeta$ (global `torch.manual_seed`) when $\tau > 0$, and stopped at

$$
n_{\mathrm{tok}} \;=\; \min\Bigl(\ \min\bigl\{\,i : \texttt{"\textbackslash n\textbackslash n"} \text{ occurs in } \mathrm{DEC}(t_{1:i}) \,\bigr\},\ \ n_{\max} \Bigr),
\qquad
a \;=\; \mathrm{strip}\bigl(\mathrm{DEC}(t_{1:n_{\mathrm{tok}}})\bigr),
\tag{27}
$$

with $n_{\max} = 200$ (`max_new_tokens`).

*Why the stop.* Without it the model runs straight past the answer into its own
confidence report (the Phase-0 prompt shows it those very instructions); a
blank line is the natural boundary of a short factual answer, which keeps $a$,
$\mathbf{g}$ and $\mathbf{H}$ scoped to the answer span alone.

### 2.4 Recovering raw logits from returned scores

`generate(output_scores=True)` returns the *processed* scores $\tilde{z}_i$,
which under sampling are temperature-scaled. Because temperature is the only
transform applied (no top-$p$/top-$k$ truncation), the scaling is exactly
invertible:

$$
\tilde{z}_{i} \;=\;
\begin{cases}
z_{i}, & \tau = 0,\\
z_{i}/\tau, & \tau > 0,
\end{cases}
\qquad\Longrightarrow\qquad
z_{i} \;=\;
\begin{cases}
\tilde{z}_{i}, & \tau = 0,\\
\tau \cdot \tilde{z}_{i}, & \tau > 0 .
\end{cases}
\tag{28}
$$

*Why.* Left unscaled, the two quantities below would be artifacts of whichever
temperature a given inquiry happened to draw, rather than properties of the
model's own distribution; undoing the scaling keeps them comparable across
trials of different $\tau$.

### 2.5 Per-token log-probability and entropy

Both are read off the same recovered logits, just reduced differently — at zero
extra model cost, so no ground truth ever needs a second forward pass:

$$
g_{i} \;=\; \log\, \mathrm{softmax}(z_{i})_{\,t_{i}}
\;=\; \log P\bigl(t_i \mid t_{<i}, x_0(q)\bigr),
\qquad i = 1,\dots,n_{\mathrm{tok}},
\tag{29}
$$

$$
H_{i} \;=\; -\sum_{t \in \mathcal{V}} \mathrm{softmax}(z_{i})_{t}\,\log\,\mathrm{softmax}(z_{i})_{t} \;\;[\text{nats}],
\qquad i = 1,\dots,n_{\mathrm{tok}} .
\tag{30}
$$

*What they are.* $g_i$ is the log-probability of the token **actually
produced** at step $i$; $H_i$ is the Shannon entropy of that step's **full**
next-token distribution — every token the model could have produced there, not
just the one it did.

## 3. Phase 1 — Categorical self-report elicitation

### 3.1 Class scale, ranges and midpoints

For an observation method $\omega$ with $K$ classes, the unit interval is
divided evenly:

$$
\mathrm{rng}^{\omega}_{k} \;=\; \Bigl[\ \tfrac{k-1}{K},\ \tfrac{k}{K}\ \Bigr),
\qquad
m^{\omega}_{k} \;=\; \frac{k - \tfrac{1}{2}}{K},
\qquad k = 1,\dots,K,
\tag{31}
$$

(both rounded to three decimals in code). For $K = 10$ this reproduces the
paper's canonical confidence ranges $0.0\text{–}0.1,\ \dots,\ 0.9\text{–}1.0$
and midpoints $0.05,0.15,\dots,0.95$ exactly.

*What it is.* $\mathrm{rng}^{\omega}_{k}$ is the numeric range printed beside
class $k$ in the prompt; $m^{\omega}_{k}$ is the scalar the class is mapped to
whenever a self-report has to be compared with a $[0,1]$-valued ground truth.

Confidence uses the paper's verbatim 10-class wording; every other construct is
built from a shared intensity ladder

$$
\mathrm{LAD} = (\text{Not},\,\text{Barely},\,\text{Slightly},\,\text{Somewhat},\,\text{Moderately},\,\text{Fairly},\,\text{Quite},\,\text{Very},\,\text{Highly},\,\text{Extremely}),
$$

$$
c^{\omega}_{k} \;=\;
\begin{cases}
\texttt{"Not "} \oplus \mathrm{adj}(\omega) \oplus \texttt{" at all"}, & k = 1,\\
\mathrm{LAD}_{k} \oplus \texttt{" "} \oplus \mathrm{adj}(\omega), & k = 2,\dots,10,
\end{cases}
\tag{32}
$$

with $\mathrm{adj}(\omega) \in \{$committed, nuanced, unwilling, varied, even$\}$.

*Why.* A single shared $K$ and framing style across all constructs means a
self-report value from any $\omega$ lives on the same scale as any other, so
correlations are comparable across the sweep; only confidence deviates,
because its wording is fixed by the paper.

### 3.2 Re-inserting the answer

Phase 1 is a **fresh** forward pass in which the model's own Phase-0 answer is
supplied as text. From trial $\theta = (s,(q,\tau,\zeta),a,\mathbf{g},\mathbf{H})$:

$$
q'(\theta) \;=\; \texttt{"Question: "} \oplus q \oplus \texttt{"\textbackslash n**Answer**: "} \oplus a,
\qquad
\mathcal{I}'(\theta) \;=\; \bigl(q'(\theta),\ 0,\ \bot\bigr).
\tag{33}
$$

*Why.* The observation method itself only ever consumes a bare inquiry, so
re-insertion belongs here; supplying the answer as context yields the same
representation at PANL as autoregressive generation would, which is what makes
Phase 0 and Phase 1 independent, separately cacheable passes.

### 3.3 The self-report prompt and its studied character offsets

With the class list rendered as
$\texttt{- "}c^{\omega}_{k}\texttt{" (}\mathrm{lo}_k\texttt{-}\mathrm{hi}_k\texttt{)}$
lines, the instruction block and full prompt are

$$
\mathrm{Instr}_{\omega} = \texttt{"Classify your }\,\mathrm{name}(\omega)\,\texttt{ ... based on }\,\mathrm{crit}(\omega)\,\texttt{ ..."} \oplus \{\text{class list}\} \oplus \cdots \oplus \underbrace{\texttt{"**}\mathrm{Name}(\omega)\texttt{**: \$CLASS"}}_{\text{format example}} \oplus \cdots,
$$

$$
\Pi_{\omega}(\theta) \;=\; \mathrm{HDR} \oplus q'(\theta) \oplus \texttt{"\textbackslash n"} \oplus \mathrm{Instr}_{\omega},
\tag{34}
$$

whose two studied newline offsets follow structurally:

$$
o_{\mathrm{PANL}} = |\mathrm{HDR}| + |q'(\theta)|,
\qquad
o_{\mathrm{PQNL}} = |\mathrm{HDR}| + \mathrm{idx}\bigl(q'(\theta),\ \texttt{"\textbackslash n**Answer**:"}\bigr),
\tag{35}
$$

where $\mathrm{idx}(u,w)$ is the character index of the first occurrence of $w$
in $u$. PANL is the newline right after the question/answer block; PQNL is the
newline right after the question, before `**Answer**:`.

*Why structural.* Both are located by construction from (33)–(34) rather than
passed in, so a prompt not built as `Question: …\n**Answer**: …` fails loudly
instead of silently mislocating a position.

### 3.4 The forced-cue read

Reading logits (rather than generating) is valid only because the classes were
engineered to have distinct first tokens. With the cue
$\mathrm{cue}_{\omega} = \texttt{"**}\mathrm{Name}(\omega)\texttt{**:"}$ appended
*after* chat-templating,

$$
x_{\omega}(\theta) \;=\; \mathrm{TOK}\bigl(\ \underbrace{\mathrm{CHAT}\bigl(\Pi_{\omega}(\theta)\bigr)}_{=\;\hat{\Pi}_\omega(\theta)} \oplus\ \mathrm{cue}_{\omega}\ \bigr),
\qquad
\iota^{\omega}_{k} \;=\; \mathrm{TOK}\bigl(\texttt{" "} \oplus c^{\omega}_{k}\bigr)_{1},
\tag{36}
$$

subject to the **mandatory** distinctness requirement (verified per tokenizer,
not assumed)

$$
\iota^{\omega}_{k} \neq \iota^{\omega}_{k'} \quad \text{for all } k \neq k' ,
\tag{37}
$$

giving the class-logit vector, the winning class and the reported value

$$
z^{\omega}(\theta) \;=\; \bigl(\,z(x_{\omega}(\theta))_{\iota^{\omega}_{1}},\ \dots,\ z(x_{\omega}(\theta))_{\iota^{\omega}_{K}}\,\bigr) \in \mathbb{R}^{K},
$$

$$
\hat{y}_{\omega}(\theta) \;=\; \operatorname*{argmax}_{k \in \{1,\dots,K\}} z^{\omega}(\theta)_{k},
\qquad
m_{\omega}(\theta) \;=\; m^{\omega}_{\hat{y}_{\omega}(\theta)} \in [0,1].
\tag{38}
$$

*What it computes.* One forward pass, no generation: prefilling the assistant
turn mid-cue forces the read position, so the very next token is the class name
and $\hat{y}_\omega$ is an argmax over the $K$ class-initial tokens only.
$m_\omega(\theta)$ is the scalar self-report used everywhere downstream. If
(37) fails the argmax cannot distinguish two classes and nothing downstream is
meaningful.

### 3.5 Token-position validity checks

Let $u$ be a rendered text and $\mathrm{tokidx}_{u}(o)$ the index of the unique
token whose offset span contains character $o$:

$$
\mathrm{tokidx}_{u}(o) \;=\; \text{the } i \text{ with } b_i \le o < e_i, \qquad (b_i,e_i) = \mathrm{OFF}(u)_i .
\tag{39}
$$

A studied newline at offset $o$ is **isolated** iff

$$
\mathrm{Iso}(u,o) \;=\;
\mathbf{1}\Bigl[\ u_{o} = \texttt{"\textbackslash n"}\ \wedge\ b_{j} = o \ \wedge\ \mathrm{DEC}(u_j) \text{ is pure whitespace beginning with } \texttt{"\textbackslash n"} \ \Bigr],
\qquad j = \mathrm{tokidx}_{u}(o).
\tag{40}
$$

*What it enforces.* The token must *start* exactly at $o$ (not be merged into
preceding content, as when an answer ends in punctuation and produces a single
`".\n"` token) and must not have swallowed what follows (as in a blank-line
`"\n\n"` token). Applied to the chat-templated text at
$o_{\mathrm{PANL}} + \delta$ and $o_{\mathrm{PQNL}} + \delta$, where
$\delta = \mathrm{idx}(\hat{\Pi}_\omega(\theta), \Pi_\omega(\theta))$ shifts the
raw offsets (35) into the templated string.

The two positions are treated **asymmetrically**, matching the guidebook:

$$
\text{PANL: } \mathrm{Iso}\bigl(\hat{\Pi}_{\omega}(\theta),\ o_{\mathrm{PANL}} + \delta\bigr) = 1 \ \ \text{required (raises otherwise)};
\qquad
\text{PQNL: } \mathrm{Iso}\bigl(\hat{\Pi}_{\omega}(\theta),\ o_{\mathrm{PQNL}} + \delta\bigr) \in \{0,1\} \ \ \text{reported only}.
\tag{41}
$$

*Why asymmetric.* PANL is mandatory — no position-level analysis is valid
without it. PQNL is exploratory and is *expected* to fail for most naturally
phrased questions, since a trailing `?`/`.` merges with the following newline
on this tokenizer.

## 4. Ground truths

Every $\gamma$ below is an **absolute** property of a single trial (never
relative to other trials in the pool), so $\gamma(\theta)$ is well defined
alone; batching only amortizes shared setup.

### 4.1 Answer sequence probability

$$
\gamma_{\mathrm{prob}}(\theta) \;=\;
\begin{cases}
\exp\Bigl(\sum_{i=1}^{n_{\mathrm{tok}}} g_{i}\Bigr) = \prod_{i=1}^{n_{\mathrm{tok}}} P\bigl(t_i \mid t_{<i}\bigr), & n_{\mathrm{tok}} \ge 1,\\
0, & n_{\mathrm{tok}} = 0 .
\end{cases}
\tag{42}
$$

*What it computes.* The probability of the whole answer sequence the model
actually produced. Each factor is a probability in $[0,1]$ by construction, so
the product is too and needs no cross-trial context (unlike a raw
log-probability, which is unbounded). Being a raw, non-length-normalized
product, it is dominated by answer length as much as by per-token confidence —
a deliberate property of "sequence probability" as a quantity, not a defect to
normalize away.

### 4.2 Answer entropy

$$
\gamma_{\mathrm{ent}}(\theta) \;=\; \sum_{i=1}^{n_{\mathrm{tok}}} H_{i} \;=\; H\bigl(t_{1:n_{\mathrm{tok}}}\bigr) \;=\; \sum_{i=1}^{n_{\mathrm{tok}}} H\bigl(t_i \mid t_{<i}\bigr).
\tag{43}
$$

*What it computes.* By the chain rule, summing the per-step conditional
entropies (30) gives exactly the entropy of the distribution over whole answer
sequences from this one prompt. Higher means the model had more live
alternatives at each step (less committed to this particular answer); lower
means it saw few real alternatives. Note this is raw summed nats — unbounded
above, unlike every other ground truth here.

### 4.3 Correctness

Answer normalization and the string-match fallback grader:

$$
\mathrm{nrm}_{a}(u) \;=\; \text{whitespace-collapsed}\bigl(\text{strip articles }\{\text{a},\text{an},\text{the}\}\text{ and non-alphanumerics from } \mathrm{lower}(u)\bigr),
\tag{44}
$$

$$
\mathrm{Match}(a, A) \;=\; \mathbf{1}\Bigl[\ \mathrm{nrm}_{a}(a) \neq \varnothing \ \wedge\ \exists\, \alpha \in A:\ \mathrm{nrm}_{a}(\alpha) \neq \varnothing\ \wedge\ \bigl(\mathrm{nrm}_{a}(\alpha) \subseteq_{\mathrm{sub}} \mathrm{nrm}_{a}(a) \ \vee\ \mathrm{nrm}_{a}(a) \subseteq_{\mathrm{sub}} \mathrm{nrm}_{a}(\alpha)\bigr)\ \Bigr],
\tag{45}
$$

where $A$ is the seed's alias tuple ($A = (e)$ when a seed carries only a
single reference answer), $\subseteq_{\mathrm{sub}}$ is substring containment,
and $\varnothing$ the empty string. The grader actually used is selected by
key availability:

$$
\mathrm{Grade}(q,a,A) \;=\;
\begin{cases}
\mathbf{1}\bigl[\ \text{GPT-4o-mini}(q,a,A) \text{ replies } \texttt{CORRECT}\ \bigr] & \text{if } \texttt{OPENAI\_API\_KEY} \text{ is set},\\
\mathrm{Match}(a, A) & \text{otherwise,}
\end{cases}
\qquad
\gamma_{\mathrm{corr}}(\theta) = \mathrm{Grade}\bigl(q,\, a,\, A(s)\bigr) \in \{0,1\}.
\tag{46}
$$

*What it computes.* Whether the trial's answer is right. The LLM judge is a
single deterministic ($\text{temperature} = 0$) call returning a bare
`CORRECT`/`INCORRECT` token, fed **all** gold aliases, as in the paper;
$\mathrm{Match}$ is the documented fallback for running without an API key, and
which one ran is recorded rather than presented silently as the other. All
grades are memoized on the key $(q, a, \mathrm{sort}(A))$.

### 4.4 Challenge (resistance to adversarial evidence)

The evidence presented is chosen **per trial**, so that it always argues
against what the model currently believes:

$$
\mathrm{Ev}(\theta) \;=\;
\begin{cases}
\text{a freshly generated counterfeit claim } \tilde{c}, & \mathrm{Match}\bigl(a, A(s)\bigr) = 1 \ \ (\text{model was right}),\\[0.5ex]
\texttt{"According to reliable reference sources, the correct answer is } e \texttt{."}, & \mathrm{Match}\bigl(a, A(s)\bigr) = 0 \ \ (\text{model was wrong}),
\end{cases}
\tag{47}
$$

with $\tilde{c}$ sampled from the same model at temperature $0.7$, at most 40
new tokens, asked for one confident sentence claiming a specific answer
different from $e$.

Let $\Pi_{\mathrm{chal}}(\theta, \mathrm{ev})$ be the challenge prompt
`"Question: q\nAnswer: a\n\n" ⊕ (ev ⊕ "\n\n") ⊕ 'Do you want to change your answer? Answer with only "Yes" or "No".'`,
chat-templated and then suffixed with the forced cue `Answer:`. With
$\iota_{\texttt{Yes}} = \mathrm{TOK}(\texttt{" Yes"})_1$ and
$\iota_{\texttt{No}} = \mathrm{TOK}(\texttt{" No"})_1$, the **defend margin** is

$$
M(\theta, \mathrm{ev}) \;=\; z\bigl(\mathrm{TOK}(\Pi_{\mathrm{chal}}(\theta,\mathrm{ev}))\bigr)_{\iota_{\texttt{No}}} \;-\; z\bigl(\mathrm{TOK}(\Pi_{\mathrm{chal}}(\theta,\mathrm{ev}))\bigr)_{\iota_{\texttt{Yes}}},
\tag{48}
$$

and the ground truth is the sigmoid-squashed margin drop:

$$
\gamma_{\mathrm{chal}}(\theta) \;=\; \mathrm{logistic}\Bigl(\ \underbrace{M(\theta, \varnothing)}_{\text{baseline, no evidence}} \;-\; \underbrace{M\bigl(\theta, \mathrm{Ev}(\theta)\bigr)}_{\text{challenged}} \ \Bigr) \;\in\; (0,1).
\tag{49}
$$

*What it computes.* $M$ is how strongly the model favors `No` ("I will not
change my answer") over `Yes`, read off logits at a forced position. The
argument of the logistic is the **decrease** in that margin caused by the
evidence, so as written $\gamma_{\mathrm{chal}}$ is **increasing in how much
the model caved**: $> 0.5$ when the evidence moved it toward changing its
answer, $< 0.5$ when the challenge left it more entrenched. (The class
docstring describes the opposite polarity — "higher means more resistant"; the
formula above is what the code computes.)

### 4.5 List variance

The free-text response is first parsed into items: one per non-empty line if
the response has more than one line, else split on `,`/`;`, each with a leading
numbering/bullet marker (`1.`, `2)`, `-`, `*`) stripped:

$$
\mathrm{Items}(a) \;=\; \bigl(\,\mathrm{it}_1,\dots,\mathrm{it}_{n_{\mathrm{it}}}\,\bigr).
\tag{50}
$$

Each item is embedded, trying the underscore-joined phrase form first (the
GoogleNews vocabulary joins common phrases with underscores, never spaces),
then falling back to averaging its constituent words' vectors:

$$
\mathrm{emb}(\mathrm{it}) \;=\;
\begin{cases}
E\bigl[\,\text{"}w_1\_\cdots\_w_r\text{"}\,\bigr], & \text{if that phrase key exists},\\[0.5ex]
\dfrac{1}{|\{j : w_j \in \mathrm{dom}(E)\}|}\displaystyle\sum_{j:\,w_j \in \mathrm{dom}(E)} E[w_j], & \text{else if any } w_j \in \mathrm{dom}(E),\\[2ex]
\bot, & \text{otherwise},
\end{cases}
\qquad (w_1,\dots,w_r) = \mathrm{lower}(\mathrm{it}).\mathrm{split}()
\tag{51}
$$

Writing $V(\theta) = \bigl(\mathrm{emb}(\mathrm{it}) : \mathrm{emb}(\mathrm{it}) \neq \bot\bigr)$
and $n_{v} = |V(\theta)|$, the spread is the mean pairwise cosine **distance**,
rescaled into $[0,1]$:

$$
\gamma_{\mathrm{lvar}}(\theta) \;=\;
\begin{cases}
\min\left(\max\left(\dfrac{1}{2}\cdot\dfrac{2}{n_{v}(n_{v}-1)} \displaystyle\sum_{1 \le i < j \le n_{v}} \bigl[\,1 - \cos\bigl(V_i, V_j\bigr)\,\bigr],\ 0\right),\ 1\right), & n_{v} \ge 2,\\[3ex]
0, & n_{v} < 2 .
\end{cases}
\tag{52}
$$

*What it computes.* How spread out the generated list is in embedding space.
Raw cosine distance lies in $[0,2]$ (0 = every item points the same direction,
2 = diametrically opposed), so the $/2$ maps it onto the same $[0,1]$ scale as
every other ground truth; the clamp guards floating-point overshoot.

### 4.6 Impurity (twenty questions)

The model's own newly proposed question (the trial's response $a$) is used to
partition the game's **full** candidate universe $\mathcal{K}$, via a fresh
partition-classification call $u$ and the parser (19):

$$
\pi_{\mathrm{yes}} = \frac{\bigl|\mathrm{Yes}(u, \mathcal{K})\bigr|}{|\mathcal{K}|},
\qquad
\pi_{\mathrm{no}} = \frac{\bigl|\mathrm{No}(u, \mathcal{K})\bigr|}{|\mathcal{K}|} = 1 - \pi_{\mathrm{yes}},
$$

$$
\gamma_{\mathrm{imp}}(\theta) \;=\; 1 - \bigl(\pi_{\mathrm{yes}}^{2} + \pi_{\mathrm{no}}^{2}\bigr) \;=\; 2\,\pi_{\mathrm{yes}}\bigl(1 - \pi_{\mathrm{yes}}\bigr) \;\in\; [0,\ 0.5].
\tag{53}
$$

*What it computes.* The Gini impurity of the induced yes/no split. $0$ = every
candidate falls on the same side (a useless question); $0.5$ = a perfect 50/50
split, the maximum attainable for a two-way partition and exactly the target
that the paired self-report's strategic framing asks the model to aim for.
Scoring against the whole universe $\mathcal{K}$ (not the set still live at
that turn) is a deliberate choice: a played game records only its
`(question, answer)` history, not per-turn remaining-set snapshots.

### 4.7 Temperature (control)

$$
\gamma_{\mathrm{temp}}(\theta) \;=\; \tau(s) \;\in\; [0,\,0.8).
\tag{54}
$$

*What it is.* The sampling temperature stored on the list-elicitation seed,
read back verbatim — not computed from the response at all. It is a **control
column, not a ground truth about model behavior**: $\tau$ is drawn before
generation and the model never sees or is told it, so any genuine self-report
correlating against it should read approximately zero. A nonzero correlation
would flag a leak (the sampling temperature biasing the response in a way the
self-report picks up on), not a calibration signal.

## 5. Calibration analysis (Experiment 0)

### 5.1 Rank correlation

For paired samples $(u_i, w_i)_{i=1}^{N}$ let $\mathrm{rk}(\cdot)$ be the
midrank transform. Spearman's rank correlation is Pearson's correlation of the
ranks:

$$
\rho(u, w) \;=\; \frac{\sum_{i=1}^{N}\bigl(\mathrm{rk}(u_i) - \overline{\mathrm{rk}(u)}\bigr)\bigl(\mathrm{rk}(w_i) - \overline{\mathrm{rk}(w)}\bigr)}
{\sqrt{\sum_{i=1}^{N}\bigl(\mathrm{rk}(u_i) - \overline{\mathrm{rk}(u)}\bigr)^{2}}\ \sqrt{\sum_{i=1}^{N}\bigl(\mathrm{rk}(w_i) - \overline{\mathrm{rk}(w)}\bigr)^{2}}}
\;\;\xrightarrow[\text{no ties}]{}\;\; 1 - \frac{6\sum_{i} d_i^{2}}{N(N^{2}-1)} .
\tag{55}
$$

*Why rank-based.* The self-report is an ordinal 10-class scale and the ground
truths live on wildly different scales (a raw sequence probability, unbounded
summed nats, a $[0,0.5]$ impurity), so only the monotone relationship is
meaningful. $\rho$ is undefined ($\mathrm{NaN}$) when either vector is constant
— the case that renders as `n/a` in the heatmaps.

### 5.2 The observation × ground-truth matrix

For a dataset $D$, an ordered list of observation methods
$(\omega_1,\dots,\omega_{R})$ and of ground truths $(\gamma_1,\dots,\gamma_{S})$,
generate the trial pool $\Theta = (\theta_1,\dots,\theta_{N})$ by (22) + §2 and
compute

$$
M_{rs} \;=\; \rho\Bigl(\ \bigl(m_{\omega_{r}}(\theta_i)\bigr)_{i=1}^{N},\ \ \bigl(\gamma_{s}(\theta_i)\bigr)_{i=1}^{N}\ \Bigr),
\qquad M \in [-1,1]^{R \times S}.
\tag{56}
$$

*What it computes.* One heatmap cell per (self-report, computed quantity) pair,
over the same trials. Row $r$ is $\omega_r$'s self-reported midpoint (38);
column $s$ is $\gamma_s$'s computed value (§4).

The **sentiment pairing** singles out which cells are the theoretically matched
diagonal — a self-report and its computed counterpart for the same underlying
idea:

$$
\mathrm{Pair} \;=\; \bigl\{\,(\omega_{\mathrm{conf}}, \gamma_{\mathrm{corr}}),\ (\omega_{\mathrm{comm}}, \gamma_{\mathrm{prob}}),\ (\omega_{\mathrm{nuan}}, \gamma_{\mathrm{ent}}),\ (\omega_{\mathrm{chal}}, \gamma_{\mathrm{chal}}),\ (\omega_{\mathrm{var}}, \gamma_{\mathrm{lvar}}),\ (\omega_{\mathrm{imp}}, \gamma_{\mathrm{imp}})\,\bigr\}.
\tag{57}
$$

*What it asserts.* "Is the answer right?", "how strongly did the model commit to
this exact answer sequence?", "how many genuinely different answers were
available?", "does it defend its answer under pressure?", "how varied is the
list?", "how evenly does the question split the candidates?" — each asked once
as a self-report and once as a computation. The four experiments run

$$
\begin{aligned}
\text{Exp 1: }&\ (\omega_{\mathrm{conf}},\omega_{\mathrm{comm}},\omega_{\mathrm{nuan}}) \times (\gamma_{\mathrm{corr}},\gamma_{\mathrm{prob}},\gamma_{\mathrm{ent}}) &&\text{over } D_{\mathrm{syn}},\, D_{\mathrm{tqa}},\\
\text{Exp 2: }&\ (\omega_{\mathrm{conf}},\omega_{\mathrm{comm}},\omega_{\mathrm{chal}},\omega_{\mathrm{nuan}}) \times (\gamma_{\mathrm{corr}},\gamma_{\mathrm{prob}},\gamma_{\mathrm{chal}},\gamma_{\mathrm{ent}}) &&\text{over } D_{\mathrm{syn}},\, D_{\mathrm{tqa}},\\
\text{Exp 3: }&\ (\omega_{\mathrm{var}}) \times (\gamma_{\mathrm{lvar}}, \gamma_{\mathrm{temp}}) &&\text{over } D_{\mathrm{list}},\\
\text{Exp 4: }&\ (\omega_{\mathrm{imp}}) \times (\gamma_{\mathrm{imp}}) &&\text{over } D_{\mathrm{20q}} .
\end{aligned}
\tag{58}
$$

Experiment 3 additionally splices temperature in as a *row* as well as a
column, using its own values in place of a self-report (there is no model
self-report to elicit for it either way):

$$
m_{\text{"temperature"}}(\theta) \;\equiv\; \gamma_{\mathrm{temp}}(\theta)
\qquad\Longrightarrow\qquad
\rho\bigl(m_{\text{"temperature"}},\, \gamma_{\mathrm{temp}}\bigr) = 1 .
\tag{59}
$$

*Why.* The $(\text{temperature},\text{temperature})$ cell is a trivial identity
check on the plumbing; the informative addition is
$\rho(\gamma_{\mathrm{temp}}, \gamma_{\mathrm{lvar}})$ — whether the seed
temperature correlates with the **actually computed** variance, not just with
the model's self-reported variety.

### 5.3 Within-keyword temperature effect

Pooling across keywords mixes their different baseline diversities, which can
swamp a real within-keyword temperature effect. Partitioning the list trials by
keyword, $\Theta_{w} = \{\theta \in \Theta : w(s(\theta)) = w\}$, gives

$$
\rho_{w} \;=\; \rho\Bigl(\ \bigl(\gamma_{\mathrm{temp}}(\theta)\bigr)_{\theta \in \Theta_{w}},\ \bigl(\gamma_{\mathrm{lvar}}(\theta)\bigr)_{\theta \in \Theta_{w}}\ \Bigr),
\qquad |\Theta_{w}| = T_{\mathrm{temp}},
\tag{60}
$$

summarized by the sign-consistency count and the mean per-keyword coefficient

$$
n_{+} \;=\; \sum_{w} \mathbf{1}\bigl[\rho_{w} > 0\bigr],
\qquad
\bar{\rho} \;=\; \frac{1}{|\{w\}|}\sum_{w} \rho_{w}.
\tag{61}
$$

*Why.* $\rho_w$ controls for keyword identity by construction (each keyword
contributes $T_{\mathrm{temp}}$ trials differing only in $\tau$ and $\zeta$);
$n_{+}$ and $\bar{\rho}$ test whether an effect hidden inside a null pooled
number is at least consistent in sign across keywords.

## 6. Activation steering (Experiment 1 / Phase 2)

### 6.1 Position token indices

Within the tokenization of the cue-suffixed self-report text
$x_\omega(\theta)$ of (36), with $\delta$ the template shift of §3.5 and
$\mathrm{FCC}$ located structurally as the colon of the format example
`**Name**: $CLASS` inside $\mathrm{Instr}_\omega$:

$$
\begin{aligned}
p_{\mathrm{PANL}}(\theta) &= \mathrm{tokidx}_{\hat{\Pi}_{\omega}(\theta)}\bigl(o_{\mathrm{PANL}} + \delta\bigr), \qquad &
p_{\mathrm{PANL{+}1}}(\theta) &= p_{\mathrm{PANL}}(\theta) + 1,\\
p_{\mathrm{FCC}}(\theta) &= \mathrm{tokidx}_{\hat{\Pi}_{\omega}(\theta)}\Bigl(\underbrace{o_{\mathrm{PANL}} + 1}_{\text{start of } \mathrm{Instr}_{\omega}} + \mathrm{idx}\bigl(\mathrm{Instr}_{\omega}, \texttt{"**}\mathrm{Name}\texttt{**: \$CLASS"}\bigr) + \mathrm{idx}(\cdot, \texttt{":"}) + \delta\Bigr), \qquad &
p_{\mathrm{CC}}(\theta) &= \bigl|x_{\omega}(\theta)\bigr| - 1 .
\end{aligned}
\tag{62}
$$

*What they are.* PANL is the key, falsifying position; PANL+1 its mandatory
one-token-away control (must show nothing); FCC a secondary control inside the
instruction block; CC the verbalization site — always the final token, exactly
where the class logits (38) are read from, so it needs no offset tracking. PANL
and FCC indices are computed on the text *without* the cue and reused with it,
which is safe because BPE tokenization is prefix-stable well away from an edit
site and both positions sit far from the end.

### 6.2 Residual capture

$$
h^{(\ell)}_{p}(\theta;\omega) \;=\; h^{(\ell)}_{\,p_{\ast}(\theta)}\bigl(x_{\omega}(\theta)\bigr) \in \mathbb{R}^{d},
\qquad p_{\ast} \text{ the index map of (62) for label } p,
\qquad p \in \mathcal{P},\ \ \ell \in \Lambda,
\tag{63}
$$

with $\Lambda = \{0,4,8,12,16,20,24,27\}$ (`steering.layers`) the swept layer
set. A single forward pass with a hook; no generation.

### 6.3 Optional ground-truth restriction of the extraction pool

Given an extraction pool $\Theta_{\mathrm{ext}}$ and a filter ground truth
$\gamma$, keep its **top (better) half by rank**:

$$
\Theta_{\mathrm{ext}}^{\gamma} \;=\; \Bigl\{\,\theta_{(i)} \;:\; i \le \bigl\lceil |\Theta_{\mathrm{ext}}|/2 \bigr\rceil \,\Bigr\},
\qquad \gamma(\theta_{(1)}) \ge \gamma(\theta_{(2)}) \ge \dots ,
\tag{64}
$$

requiring $|\Theta_{\mathrm{ext}}^{\gamma}| \ge 2$.

*Why a relative split, not a fixed threshold.* The paper needs this only for
correctness, where values are exactly $\{0,1\}$ and a $\ge 0.5$ cut reduces to
"keep the 1s". But $\gamma_{\mathrm{imp}} \le 0.5$ always and
$\gamma_{\mathrm{ent}}$ is unbounded raw nats, so a fixed $\ge 0.5$ threshold
would silently keep almost nothing or almost everything for those. A rank split
means "this pool's own better half" regardless of the ground truth's range.
$\gamma = \bot$ skips filtering entirely.

### 6.4 Steering-vector construction

Rank the (possibly filtered) pool $\Theta_{\mathrm{ext}}^{\gamma}$ by the
observation method's **own clean self-report** — never by a ground truth, which
plays no role in this experiment:

$$
m_{\omega}(\theta_{[1]}) \le m_{\omega}(\theta_{[2]}) \le \dots \le m_{\omega}(\theta_{[P]}),
\qquad P = \bigl|\Theta_{\mathrm{ext}}^{\gamma}\bigr|,
\qquad
n_{\mathrm{top}}' = \max\Bigl(1,\ \min\bigl(n_{\mathrm{top}},\ \lfloor P/2 \rfloor\bigr)\Bigr),
\tag{65}
$$

$$
\mathcal{L} = \bigl\{\theta_{[1]},\dots,\theta_{[n_{\mathrm{top}}']}\bigr\},
\qquad
\mathcal{H} = \bigl\{\theta_{[P - n_{\mathrm{top}}' + 1]},\dots,\theta_{[P]}\bigr\},
\qquad \mathcal{H} \cap \mathcal{L} = \emptyset .
\tag{66}
$$

With $\mu(\cdot)$ the mean residual activation over a trial set and
$\bar{n}^{(\ell,p)}$ the mean residual norm over the whole (filtered) pool,

$$
\bar{n}^{(\ell,p)} \;=\; \frac{1}{P}\sum_{\theta \in \Theta_{\mathrm{ext}}^{\gamma}} \bigl\lVert h^{(\ell)}_{p}(\theta;\omega)\bigr\rVert,
\qquad
v_{0}^{(\ell,p)} \;=\; \mu\bigl(\mathcal{H}\bigr) - \mu\bigl(\mathcal{L}\bigr),
$$

$$
\boxed{\;
v^{(\ell,p)} \;=\; \frac{v_{0}^{(\ell,p)}}{\bigl\lVert v_{0}^{(\ell,p)}\bigr\rVert}\;\cdot\;0.03\;\bar{n}^{(\ell,p)}
\;}
\tag{67}
$$

*What it computes.* The paper's own construction $v_{\mathrm{high}} = \mu(H) - \mu(L)$,
$v_{\mathrm{low}} = -v_{\mathrm{high}}$, scaled to **3% of the mean residual
norm** at that (layer, position) — generalized here from confidence to any
Likert construct. $n_{\mathrm{top}} = 10$ (`steering.top_n`) is far below the
paper's 25, since this pool is nowhere near its 3,000-trial activation set; the
clamp in (65) guarantees $\mathcal{H}$ and $\mathcal{L}$ never overlap.
$\Theta_{\mathrm{ext}}$ is disjoint from the test pool by (23), so no trial's
own activation contributes to the vector that later steers it.

### 6.5 Class-balanced test set

From the oversampled candidate pool $\Theta_{\mathrm{tc}}$
($|\Theta_{\mathrm{tc}}| = n_{\mathrm{tc}} \gg n_{\mathrm{test}}$), ranked
ascending by $m_{\omega}$ as in (65):

$$
b = \Bigl\lfloor \tfrac{n_{\mathrm{test}}}{2} \Bigr\rfloor,
\qquad
\Theta_{\mathrm{test}} \;=\; \underbrace{\bigl\{\theta_{[1]},\dots,\theta_{[b]}\bigr\}}_{\text{lowest-reported}} \;\cup\; \underbrace{\bigl\{\theta_{[\,|\Theta_{\mathrm{tc}}| - (n_{\mathrm{test}} - b) + 1]},\dots,\theta_{[\,|\Theta_{\mathrm{tc}}|]}\bigr\}}_{\text{highest-reported}},
\tag{68}
$$

subject to the non-degeneracy requirement
$m_{\omega}(\theta_{[b]}) \neq m_{\omega}(\theta_{[\,|\Theta_{\mathrm{tc}}| - (n_{\mathrm{test}} - b) + 1]})$.

*Why relative rather than the paper's fixed top-3/bottom-3 classes.* The model
favors high-intensity classes on every Likert scale here — checked directly,
commitment landed in the bottom three of ten classes on 0 of 30 real candidates
— so a fixed-threshold balanced split fails outright on this model's real
distribution. A relative split always yields a genuine low-vs-high contrast
provided the pool has any spread, and the requirement above raises if it does
not. Balance matters because an unbalanced test set under-samples the low pole
and cannot test **bidirectional** steering.

### 6.6 The intervention and the steered read

At test time the residual stream is additively modulated at one (layer,
position) — the paper's $\tilde{r}^{(\ell)} = r^{(\ell)} + \alpha v^{(\ell)}$:

$$
\tilde{h}^{(\ell)}_{p} \;=\; h^{(\ell)}_{p} \;+\; \alpha\, v^{(\ell,p)},
\qquad \alpha \in \{+\alpha_0,\ -\alpha_0\},\quad \alpha_0 = 5,
\tag{69}
$$

applied only at position $p$ and only at layer $\ell$'s output; all other
positions and layers are untouched. Write

$$
z^{\omega}\bigl(\theta;\ \ell,\ p,\ \alpha\bigr) \in \mathbb{R}^{K}
\tag{70}
$$

for the resulting class-logit vector (36) read at CC under that intervention,
with $z^{\omega}(\theta;\ell,p,0) = z^{\omega}(\theta)$ the **clean** read.

*Why both signs, always.* $+\alpha_0 v$ steers toward the high pole,
$-\alpha_0 v$ toward the low pole, on the *same* test trials; "strong,
bidirectional, graded" is part of the claim being tested, not an optional
extra. Note the read is always at CC, so when $p = \mathrm{CC}$ injection and
read coincide, and when $p \in \{\mathrm{PANL}, \mathrm{PANL{+}1}, \mathrm{FCC}\}$
the effect must propagate forward through causal attention to reach CC.

### 6.7 The three metrics

Fix the clean prediction $y = \hat{y}_{\omega}(\theta)$ (i.e. the argmax of the
clean read) as the target class for **both** the clean and steered evaluations.

**(a) Self-report midpoint delta.**

$$
\Delta m\bigl(\theta;\ell,p,\alpha\bigr) \;=\; m^{\omega}_{\,\operatorname*{argmax}_{k} z^{\omega}(\theta;\ell,p,\alpha)_{k}} \;-\; m^{\omega}_{\,y} .
\tag{71}
$$

*What it measures.* The shift, in $[0,1]$ self-report units, that the
intervention causes in the verbalized class.

**(b) Logit difference and its change** (the paper's Eq. 3, and its primary
metric):

$$
\Delta_{\mathrm{logit}}\bigl(z^{\omega}, y\bigr) \;=\; z^{\omega}_{y} \;-\; \frac{1}{K-1}\sum_{k \neq y} z^{\omega}_{k},
\tag{72}
$$

$$
\delta_{\mathrm{logit}}\bigl(\theta;\ell,p,\alpha\bigr) \;=\; \Delta_{\mathrm{logit}}\bigl(z^{\omega}(\theta;\ell,p,\alpha),\ y\bigr) \;-\; \Delta_{\mathrm{logit}}\bigl(z^{\omega}(\theta),\ y\bigr).
\tag{73}
$$

*What it measures.* $\Delta_{\mathrm{logit}}$ is how strongly the model favors
class $y$ over the mean of the $K-1$ alternatives; (73) reports the **change**
in that quantity with $y$ held fixed at the clean prediction, so the metric is
a graded measure of how far the intervention pushed the model off its original
answer — not merely whether the argmax flipped.

**(c) First-token change rate** (the paper's Eq. 4):

$$
\mathrm{CR}\bigl(\ell,p,\alpha\bigr) \;=\; \frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N} \mathbf{1}\Bigl[\ \operatorname*{argmax}_{k} z^{\omega}\bigl(\theta_i;\ell,p,\alpha\bigr)_{k} \;\neq\; \operatorname*{argmax}_{k} z^{\omega}\bigl(\theta_i\bigr)_{k}\ \Bigr],
\qquad N = \bigl|\Theta_{\mathrm{test}}\bigr|.
\tag{74}
$$

*What it measures.* The fraction of test trials whose verbalized class actually
flips under intervention.

### 6.8 Per-direction aggregation

For each $(\ell, p)$ and each direction $\alpha \in \{+\alpha_0, -\alpha_0\}$,
over $\Theta_{\mathrm{test}}$:

$$
\overline{\Delta m}(\ell,p,\alpha) = \frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N} \Delta m(\theta_i;\ell,p,\alpha),
\qquad
\mathrm{SEM}(\ell,p,\alpha) = \mathrm{sem}\Bigl(\bigl\{\Delta m(\theta_i;\ell,p,\alpha)\bigr\}_{i=1}^{N}\Bigr),
$$

$$
\overline{\delta_{\mathrm{logit}}}(\ell,p,\alpha) = \frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N} \delta_{\mathrm{logit}}(\theta_i;\ell,p,\alpha).
\tag{75}
$$

*What it produces.* The plotted quantity: mean $\Delta$ (steered $-$ clean)
against layer, one curve per (position, direction), with SEM error bars — for
all four positions of $\mathcal{P}$ and both directions on the same axes. A
PANL curve indistinguishable from its own PANL+1 and FCC controls is the
informative "no effect here" reading; without a control, a flat PANL result
could not be told apart from "the whole intervention mechanism does nothing".

The five (dataset, observation method, filter ground truth) sweeps run are

$$
\begin{aligned}
&\bigl(D_{\mathrm{tqa}},\ \omega_{\mathrm{conf}},\ \gamma_{\mathrm{corr}}\bigr), \quad
\bigl(D_{\mathrm{syn}},\ \omega_{\mathrm{comm}},\ \gamma_{\mathrm{prob}}\bigr), \quad
\bigl(D_{\mathrm{syn}},\ \omega_{\mathrm{nuan}},\ \gamma_{\mathrm{ent}}\bigr),\\
&\bigl(D_{\mathrm{list}},\ \omega_{\mathrm{var}},\ \gamma_{\mathrm{lvar}}\bigr), \quad
\bigl(D_{\mathrm{20q}},\ \omega_{\mathrm{imp}},\ \gamma_{\mathrm{imp}}\bigr),
\end{aligned}
\tag{76}
$$

each over $\mathcal{P} \times \Lambda \times \{+\alpha_0, -\alpha_0\}$. The
first is the paper's own experiment; the other four are the generalization to
other self-reportable constructs, each filtered by its own sentiment-paired
counterpart (57) rather than literal answer-correctness, since none of those
four datasets has a single right/wrong answer.

### 6.9 The AC position: a different mechanism

AC lives in the **Phase-0** prompt (25) — a different forward pass entirely
from the Phase-1 prompt every position in $\mathcal{P}$ reads from. Its index
and vector are

$$
p_{\mathrm{AC}}(\theta) = \bigl|x_{0}(q)\bigr| - 1,
\qquad
h^{(\ell)}_{\mathrm{AC}}(\theta) = h^{(\ell)}_{p_{\mathrm{AC}}(\theta)}\bigl(x_{0}(q)\bigr),
\qquad
v_{\mathrm{AC}}^{(\ell)} \text{ built by (65)–(67) with } h^{(\ell)}_{\mathrm{AC}} \text{ in place of } h^{(\ell)}_{p} .
\tag{77}
$$

Because Phase 1 never continues Phase 0's residual stream (the answer is
re-inserted as *text*), an AC intervention has exactly one channel to the
confidence read: changing what answer gets generated in the first place. So
testing AC requires a real **regeneration** under intervention, not a single
forward pass:

$$
\tilde{a}(\theta;\ell,\alpha) \;=\; \text{greedy decode of } x_{0}(q) \text{ with } \tilde{h}^{(\ell)}_{p_{\mathrm{AC}}} = h^{(\ell)}_{p_{\mathrm{AC}}} + \alpha\, v_{\mathrm{AC}}^{(\ell)} \text{ applied only while } |x| > p_{\mathrm{AC}},
\tag{78}
$$

$$
\tilde{\theta}(\theta;\ell,\alpha) \;=\; \bigl(s,\ \mathcal{I},\ \tilde{a}(\theta;\ell,\alpha),\ \mathbf{g},\ \mathbf{H}\bigr).
\tag{79}
$$

*Why the guard.* The hook fires on every decode step, but only the first
(prefill) step still contains the full prompt; every later step's output is a
single new token, and indexing at $p_{\mathrm{AC}}$ there would either error or
corrupt a just-generated token instead of leaving decoding alone.

The effect is then read with a **plain, unsteered** self-report on the
regenerated trial, so the same three metrics of §6.7 apply and remain directly
comparable with (71)–(75):

$$
\Delta m_{\mathrm{AC}}(\theta;\ell,\alpha) = m_{\omega}\bigl(\tilde{\theta}(\theta;\ell,\alpha)\bigr) - m_{\omega}(\theta),
\qquad
\delta_{\mathrm{logit}}^{\mathrm{AC}}(\theta;\ell,\alpha) = \Delta_{\mathrm{logit}}\bigl(z^{\omega}(\tilde{\theta}),\,y\bigr) - \Delta_{\mathrm{logit}}\bigl(z^{\omega}(\theta),\,y\bigr),
\tag{80}
$$

with $y = \hat{y}_{\omega}(\theta)$ again fixed at the clean prediction, and
$\mathrm{CR}$, $\overline{\Delta m}$, $\mathrm{SEM}$ formed exactly as in (74)
and (75).

*Why kept separate.* Two full regenerations per test trial, per layer, is a
categorically higher cost than the single forward pass every position in
$\mathcal{P}$ needs — which is why AC is a small, separate check rather than
part of the dense per-layer, dual-direction sweep.
