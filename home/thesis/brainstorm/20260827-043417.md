3. **An extension is what gives RQ1/RQ4 real teeth**, because it's the one experiment whose *outcome* could differentiate hypotheses: if the internal/verbal dissociation generalizes to other epistemic states (surprise, hedging), that's evidence for "this is a general cache-then-verbalize architecture," pointing away from "confidence is a special introspective state" and toward the observer-relative/linguistic-performance reading. If it *doesn't* generalize, that's evidence confidence is doing something distinctive. Either outcome is a real, thesis-original finding to reason from — pure replication can't produce that.

Recommended keeping the extension deliberately minimal — one additional state (surprise is easiest to operationalize: compare cached signal against an objective surprise proxy like predictive perplexity or expectation-mismatch, rather than something fuzzier like hedging), one model, one dataset — rather than trying to match the full replication's breadth.

5. **Expectation-setting on the philosophical payoff**: recommended not overpromising what the thesis can conclude about "what confidence IS."
