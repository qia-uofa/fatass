# Bachelorarbeit Format Guide — Informatik, Goethe-Universität Frankfurt

**⚠ Timing note:** your officially issued Abgabetermin is 31.08.2026 — the same date as this guide was produced. Everything under "Hard requirements → Submission" below is not background reading, it is today's checklist.

This guide is organized around what is actually **mandated** by the Prüfungsordnung and the Prüfungsamt Informatik versus what is **convention**. Every hard-requirement item below is cited to a specific official document I read directly (not summarized secondhand); anything I could not verify from an official source is explicitly marked unconfirmed rather than presented as a rule.

## HARD REQUIREMENTS (mandated — cited to source)

### Processing time and scope
- The Bachelorarbeit is worth 12 CP, corresponding to a 9-week Bearbeitungszeit, starting the day after the officially logged Themenausgabe. — *[PO 2019] §35 Abs. 3; [Merkblatt] p.1*
- The topic may not be worked on before the officially logged issuance date. — *[PO 2019] §35 Abs. 9*
- The topic can be returned only once, only within the first half of the processing time, and the replacement topic must differ in content and cannot itself be returned. — *[PO 2019] §35 Abs. 12*
- An extension of up to 50% of the processing time (32 days for a 9-week thesis) is possible only with a doctor's note (illness) or another unavoidable reason, filed with the Prüfungsamt **before** the deadline, and requires the supervisor's consent too. — *[PO 2019] §35 Abs. 13; [Merkblatt] p.1–2*

### Language
- Default language is **German**. A foreign language (e.g. English) requires: (a) the supervisor's written consent, and (b) confirmed availability of a second examiner with adequate language qualification in that language — and the request must be made **at the latest when the thesis is registered** (as part of the Moodle Anmeldung process). — *[PO 2019] §35 Abs. 11; [Merkblatt] p.1*
- **Inference worth noting (not independently verified):** the Merkblatt's "Titel" rule states the thesis title may only be issued in English if the thesis itself will also be written in English (see below). Since the officially issued Themenausgabe title is *"An Ontological Perspective on LLM Beliefs"* — in English — this is fairly strong indirect evidence that English-language approval was already granted at registration. This is an inference from the rule's logic, not a directly confirmed fact about this specific registration — get explicit written confirmation from the supervisor or Prüfungsamt rather than relying on this inference alone.

### Title
- The submitted title must be **verbatim identical** to the title on the Ausgabeschreiben (official topic-issuance letter). No subsequent title changes are provided for. — *[Merkblatt] p.1, "Titel"*
- Practical consequence for this thesis: any surviving "...LLM Confidence" (working-title) language anywhere in the bound document is not just stylistically stale, it is **noncompliant** — the cover, running headers, and any self-reference to the thesis title must read "An Ontological Perspective on LLM Beliefs."

### Submission — due **today**
- Submit **3 written, bound copies** plus **one electronic version**, both by the deadline. — *[PO 2019] §35 Abs. 15; [Merkblatt] p.2*
- Binding must be **firm hardcover or softcover — spiral binding is explicitly disallowed.** — *[Merkblatt] p.2* (this specific method is a Prüfungsausschuss-level specification issued under the authority of §35 Abs. 15 Satz 2, not spelled out in the PO text itself, but it is binding all the same)
- Electronic version: uploaded to Hessenbox, on the same day, via your individual Hessenbox link (sent at registration; see the Moodle-registration handbook if you've lost it). — *[Merkblatt] p.2*
- **All submitted versions (all 3 printed + the electronic one) must be identical**, and each must include: page numbers, a Zusammenfassung (abstract/summary — content, language, and length not specified by these documents), and the signed "Erklärung zur Abschlussarbeit" bound in. — *[Merkblatt] p.2*
- The signed Erklärung must **additionally** be submitted once more as a **separate loose original sheet** at the point of submission — this is on top of being bound into every copy, not instead of it. — *[PO 2019] §35 Abs. 16; [Merkblatt] p.2*
- Missing the deadline, or submitting in the wrong form, is automatically graded "nicht ausreichend" (5.0/fail) — no gray area. — *[PO 2019] §35 Abs. 14–15; [Merkblatt] p.2*
- A failed Bachelorarbeit may be repeated only once. — *[Merkblatt] p.2, citing PO §40 Abs. 4*
- If submitting outside Prüfungsamt office hours: postal submission (postmark counts as the date; registered mail/parcel recommended) or the silver mailbox in the Institut für Informatik foyer. — *[Merkblatt] p.2*

### Declaration of authorship (Erklärung zur Abschlussarbeit / Eigenständigkeitserklärung)
- Required by §35 Abs. 16. Content, per the official form *[Erklärung-Formular]* (quoted, translated from German): *"I hereby declare [name] that I have written the present work independently and without using sources or aids other than those specified. I further confirm that the present work has not been used, even in part, for another examination or course requirement. I also assure that all submitted bound written copies of my thesis match the digitally submitted electronic version."* Hand-signed, dated "Frankfurt am Main, den ___."
- The thesis must mark, as such, all passages, images, and drawings taken literally or in substance from publications or other external texts. — *[PO 2019] §35 Abs. 16*
- **AI-tool use and this declaration:** the declaration's "ohne Benutzung anderer als der angegebenen Quellen und Hilfsmittel" (without using aids other than those declared) clause means any AI tool used as a writing/research aid is a "Hilfsmittel" that must be disclosed — leaving it out of the declared aids while having used it substantially would make the signed declaration false. The university's general AI guidance *[KI-Hinweise]* does not add a Fachbereich-Informatik-specific disclosure format on top of this, and I could not find one — it only says AI use in examination contexts must be discussed with the instructor/supervisor beforehand. There is **no officially mandated "Note on the Use of AI Tools" section or template** — the obligation is real and hard (it flows from the declaration itself), but the exact form of satisfying it is left to you (see Soft Requirements below for a concrete recommendation).

### Second examiner
- The Prüfungsamt assigns a second examiner (Zweitgutachter/in) automatically after submission; the supervisor is first examiner. You may propose a second examiner via a form (from Downloadbereich) or during Moodle registration, but the Prüfungsamt can deviate from your proposal. — *[PO 2019] §35 Abs. 17; [Merkblatt] p.2*

## COULD NOT VERIFY AS A HARD REQUIREMENT (do not treat these as mandated)

I read the full PO §35 text and the current Prüfungsamt Merkblatt directly (not a secondary summary) and found **no** mandated:
- **Page or word count limit.** Not mentioned anywhere in §35 or the Merkblatt. Any number you've heard quoted (e.g. "30–50 pages") is departmental/supervisor custom, not a rule — see Soft Requirements.
- **Font, font size, margins, or line spacing.** Not specified.
- **Citation style** (APA/IEEE/ACM/etc.). Not mandated.
- **Language of the Zusammenfassung** when the thesis itself is in a foreign language. (Note: the parallel Merkblatt for the *Master* Wirtschaftsinformatik degree does require a German-language summary when the thesis is in a foreign language — but I could not find that specific clause in the Bachelor Informatik PO or Merkblatt. Don't assume it transfers; ask your supervisor or the Prüfungsamt directly if you're writing in English.)
- **A specific "AI usage disclosure" section format.** As above — the obligation to disclose is real (via the Eigenständigkeitserklärung), but no specific section, heading, or wording is prescribed by any official document I could locate.
- **Mandated chapter/section structure** (e.g. a fixed Intro→Methods→Results skeleton). Confirmed absent from §35 — structure is your craft choice.

## SOFT REQUIREMENTS / CONVENTIONS (recommended, not mandated)

These are field/departmental norms, not enforceable rules. Treat them as sensible defaults you can deviate from with a reason, not as compliance items.

- **Structure:** Standard CS bachelor's envelope — Introduction, Related Work, Background, Methodology, Results, Discussion, Limitations, Conclusion — is the conventional shape for this kind of replicate-and-extend empirical thesis, and matches the argumentative spine you've already converged on (replicate → extend → discuss).
- **Length:** No official limit exists, but CS bachelor's theses in this kind of empirical/experimental track conventionally run roughly 30–50 pages of main text (excluding front matter, references, appendices). This is a general field calibration, not a Goethe-specific or Ramesh-group-specific figure I could verify — use it as a sanity check, not a target.
- **Typography:** Common defaults in German CS theses — 11–12pt serif body text, 1.15–1.5 line spacing, ~2.5cm margins, numbered headings. Not sourced to any Goethe document; pick something readable and consistent, and match whatever LaTeX/Word template your chair or lab typically uses if one exists.
- **Citation style:** Not mandated; ML/interpretability papers you're citing (Kumaran et al. 2026 and similar ICML/NeurIPS-adjacent work) typically use numeric or author-year styles (e.g. natbib). Pick one, apply it consistently, and — since this is a soft item — just confirm your supervisor's group has no house style before finalizing.
- **Front matter beyond what's mandated:** a table of contents and list of figures/tables are standard practice even though only page numbers + Zusammenfassung + Erklärung are actually required.
- **AI disclosure section (recommended concrete form):** since disclosure is a hard obligation but its format is not prescribed, add a short, factual "Note on the Use of AI Tools" section (front or back matter) stating which tools were used, for what (e.g. code scaffolding via the `fatass`/Claude pipeline, brainstorming/planning), and what remained the writer's own argument, RQs, and interpretation — consistent with your own stated commitment to disclosed, honest AI use. Point to git/`./log` history as available evidence if asked.
- **Abstract language:** given the Master-WI parallel rule (German abstract required alongside a foreign-language thesis) and the general norm in German CS departments, including a short German Zusammenfassung alongside an English one is good practice even where not confirmed as required for this program — cheap insurance either way.

## Quick pre-submission checklist (today)

- [ ] Zusammenfassung/abstract included
