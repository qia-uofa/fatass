# Andrew Carnegie - Wikipedia Article Summary

**Andrew Carnegie** (November 25, 1835 – August 11, 1919) was a Scottish-American industrialist and philanthropist who became one of the richest Americans in history.

## Early Life and Career

Carnegie was born in Dunfermline, Scotland, in a weaver's cottage. His family immigrated to Allegheny, Pennsylvania, in 1848 when he was 12 years old. He began work as a bobbin boy in a cotton mill earning $1.20 weekly, later becoming a telegraph messenger and eventually a railroad superintendent under Thomas A. Scott of the Pennsylvania Railroad.

## Business Success

Carnegie accumulated wealth through strategic investments in railroads, sleeping cars, bridges, and oil. His primary fortune came from the steel industry. He adopted the Bessemer process for efficient steel production and vertically integrated his supply chain by controlling raw materials, transportation, and manufacturing. By 1892, he formed the Carnegie Steel Company, which became the world's largest steel producer.

In 1901, J.P. Morgan purchased Carnegie's steel enterprises for $303.45 million, forming U.S. Steel Corporation. This transaction made Carnegie temporarily wealthier than John D. Rockefeller.

## Philanthropy

During his final 18 years, Carnegie donated approximately $350 million (roughly $6.9 billion in 2025 dollars)—nearly 90% of his fortune. His philanthropic initiatives included:

- **Libraries**: Funded approximately 3,000 public libraries across English-speaking countries
- **Education**: Established Carnegie Institute of Technology (now Carnegie Mellon University) and contributed to Scottish universities
- **Culture**: Built Carnegie Hall and funded 7,000 pipe organs
- **Peace**: Founded the Carnegie Endowment for International Peace and the Peace Palace in The Hague
- **Other causes**: Supported music education, scientific research, and the Carnegie Hero Fund

His 1889 essay "The Gospel of Wealth" argued that the wealthy had a moral obligation to use their fortunes for public benefit.

## Controversies

**Johnstown Flood (1889)**: Carnegie was a member of the South Fork Fishing and Hunting Club, which owned the dam that failed, causing a disaster killing over 2,200 people. Club members, including Carnegie, successfully avoided legal liability.

**Homestead Strike (1892)**: While Carnegie was in Scotland, his partner Henry Clay Frick hired Pinkerton agents to confront strikers. The violent confrontation resulted in multiple deaths and injuries, damaging Carnegie's philanthropic reputation despite his subsequent charitable contributions.

## Legacy

Carnegie died on August 11, 1919, from bronchial pneumonia. He was buried in Sleepy Hollow Cemetery in New York. His legacy includes numerous institutions bearing his name and the modern concept of philanthropic responsibility among the wealthy.
