Andrew Carnegie - Wikipedia Article Summary

Andrew Carnegie (1835-1919) was a Scottish-born American steel industrialist and philanthropist who became one of the wealthiest individuals of his era.

## Early Life

Born in Dunfermline, Scotland, Carnegie emigrated to the United States with his family in 1848, settling in Allegheny, Pennsylvania. He began working at age 13 in a cotton mill before transitioning to the telegraph industry at 14.

## Steel Industry Success

Carnegie's career took off when Thomas A. Scott of the Pennsylvania Railroad recognized his talents and made him his secretary. After the Civil War, Carnegie established his steel business, making strategic investments in iron mills and the Bessemer steel process. He constructed his first blast furnace in 1870 and established the Edgar Thomson Steel Works in 1873.

By merging with Henry Clay Frick's coke operations in 1881, Carnegie achieved significant vertical integration. When he retired from active management in 1901, he sold his company to J.P. Morgan for $480 million, which led to the formation of U.S. Steel.

## Philanthropic Legacy

Carnegie believed that "the man who dies rich, dies in disgrace." He donated over $350 million (approximately $11.9 billion in today's currency) to various causes:

- **Libraries**: Funded construction of 2,509 public libraries
- **Education**: Founded Carnegie Mellon University and Carnegie Institution of Washington
- **Peace**: Established the Carnegie Endowment for International Peace with $10 million
- **Culture**: Financed Carnegie Hall in New York

He also created the Carnegie Hero Fund Commission to honor courageous individuals.

## Criticism

Despite his philanthropy, Carnegie faced criticism for exploiting workers in his steel mills, paying minimal wages while workers endured dangerous conditions and long hours.
