# Narrative Style Guide

Distilled from a side-by-side comparison of an AI-drafted paragraph and
the user's rewrite of the same content (a summary of Benzon's "ChatGPT's
Ontological Landscape"). Concrete rules over self-report — each point
below is backed by an example from the diff.

## Sentence construction

- Break long comma-spliced or "X, and Y, and I think Z" sentences into
  two or more shorter ones.
- Sentence fragments are allowed on purpose, usually a subordinate
  clause left standing alone to isolate one claim: *"Because ideas
  aren't the kind of thing that can be colored or that can sleep."*
- Medium-length sentences predominate. Short declaratives are used for
  emphasis or restatement: *"The answer is yes."* / *"The answers were
  acceptable."*
- Don't chain evaluative asides onto a claim with "and" — cut the aside
  or give it its own sentence.

## Point of view & narration

- When narrating what a source does, stay in consistent third person,
  subject-first: *"He goes on to..."*, *"He then asks..."*, *"He then
  moves on to..."* — repeating "He [verb]" at sentence starts is the
  expected structuring device, not a flaw to vary away from.
- Cut first-person editorializing embedded mid-sentence (*"and I think
  it's the most interesting thing in the whole paper"*). If an
  evaluation is worth keeping, state it as the source's claim/thesis,
  not the writer's live reaction.
- Prefer one consistent tense for narrating what an author/paper does
  (the rewrite moves toward present-tense narration — "opens," "goes
  on," "asks," "moves on" — rather than the original's mix of present
  and past).

## Structure

- Follow the source's own sequence — "the skeleton" — rather than
  reordering material for effect.
- Move through claims in order: setup/example → finding → generalization
  or thesis → next investigation. Mark the pivot to the thesis
  explicitly (*"The refusal marks the thesis of Benzon:"*) instead of
  building to it implicitly.

## Show vs. tell

- Favor direct restatement over descriptive gloss, even at the cost of
  nuance: *"it gives answers Benzon accepts with little hesitation"*
  becomes *"The answers were acceptable."*
- State a mechanism or thesis plainly rather than dramatizing it.

## Diction

- Prefer precise/technical terms over their folksy equivalents:
  "token" over "word," "syntactically/semantically" over a loose
  "grammatical/nonsensical" pairing.
- No metaphors, figurative language, or clever cliches. A source's own
  figurative device (e.g. Waddington's "epigenetic landscape") is kept
  only as a labeled illustration of a mechanism, never extended into
  the writer's own figurative language.
